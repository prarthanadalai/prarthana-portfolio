about · 01

How I work,
from brief to ship.

A short note on what I do, how I think about product, and where I’m most useful on a team.

I’m a product manager who treats shipped, adopted as the only finished state. For 5+ years I’ve owned products from PRD to release across enterprise B2B and growth-stage B2C — partnering closely with engineering, design, sales, and customers. I work backward from a clear North Star, instrument adoption signals, and iterate against guardrails.

I’m most useful where the problem is ambiguous, the system is complex, and the rollout has to land with non‑technical users in operational environments. — a working principle

The work is rarely about the headline feature — it’s about the second screen, the exception flow, the audit log nobody asked for until they did. My job is to find the version of the product that actually gets used after the demo, then make it the obvious thing to ship next.

what i’m best at

  • Workflow systems Role-based ops, approvals, audit trails, edge cases.
  • Growth levers Incentives, adoption metrics, measurable iteration loops.
  • Execution PRD → delivery → rollout → continuous improvement.

does the loop close? ✿

Teardown
Self-internship

A first-principles teardown of an AI tutor.

Speak is a $1B AI‑native language app that teaches you to talk. I tore down its core loop firsthand to find the one place its promise quietly leaks.

Thesis
Duolingo bolts AI onto a game; Speak is the conversation.
Method
Firsthand use — onboarding, a full Korean lesson, the paywall — plus public research.
The gap
Three steps teach well; the fourth — retaining what you spoke — never closes.
The bet
Close the loop: spaced review of the day’s words, measured by 7‑day retention.
